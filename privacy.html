Memora Privacy & Data Choices

Updated October 29, 2026. Memora is an educational memory game, not a medical assessment, diagnosis or treatment.

What is stored on your device?

Memora stores your selected age range, random game nickname, experience points, streaks, completed levels and individual gameplay attempts in your browser. Each attempt may include the game world, level, correct or incorrect result, approximate response time and date/time. This lets the game remember your progress. We do not ask for your real name, email address, exact birthdate or health information.

Who can choose online saving?

Players aged 13 or younger can play locally only; Memora does not send their gameplay information to the Supabase cloud database. Players aged 14 or older can either play locally or separately choose Agree & play with cloud saving. Online saving is off by default, and declining does not prevent playing.

When you opt in, Memora sends your random nickname, age range, game progress, scores, streaks, completed levels, and individual gameplay attempts (including accuracy, approximate response times and timestamps) to Supabase. The information is linked to an anonymous authentication account ID. Nicknames and IDs are pseudonymous, not guaranteed to be anonymous.

Why do we use this information?

On-device information runs the game and keeps local progress. Optional cloud information saves your game progress and helps the Memora project owner understand gameplay patterns, check how the game works and improve it. These are the stated uses of optional cloud saving. Memora is not currently enrolling players in a formal research study or sending data to its separate research database. Any later study or new purpose would need its own safeguards, explanation and consent where required.

Where does information go, and who can see it?

GitHub Pages hosts the website, and Supabase provides optional cloud accounts and gameplay storage. The game is designed so cloud gameplay records are accessible only through the associated account or authorized project administration, not published as an individual public profile. Hosting and service providers can process technical logs, including IP addresses, and may store information outside your country. No internet service offers zero risk.

Stopping sharing or deleting data

In the game, go to Worlds → Privacy settings → Stop sending data to stop future cloud uploads from this browser. Stopping sharing does not erase data already saved online. You can choose local-only play without losing your ability to use the game.

To erase it, select Delete my saved gameplay data in Privacy settings while using the browser with access to the original cloud account, and confirm. Memora will attempt to delete the account's cloud gameplay records and progress, verify that those tables contain no remaining records for that account, stop cloud uploads, and clear local gameplay history and progress so old records cannot be uploaded again. The selected age range and nickname remain locally. The game reports an error if deletion cannot be confirmed.

This button does not remove the Supabase anonymous authentication account itself, service-provider logs, or backups subject to their policies. If you lose access to the original anonymous session (for example, by clearing the browser first), automatic deletion may no longer be possible through that device. The project owner will need a separate verified support route for such cases. Avoid posting private account identifiers publicly.

Retention and further choices

Local progress is kept until you clear it in the game or erase the site's browser storage. Cloud gameplay records remain until deletion or a future stated retention policy is applied. We will explain material changes to data uses and seek renewed consent if required. Separate research data collection remains disabled while its ethics and consent arrangements are being reviewed.

Safety and questions

Memora is a student-led educational project. Before expanding participant data collection, the project owner should publish a privacy contact and confirm appropriate safeguards for minors, research oversight, deletion requests made after browser access is lost, and retention periods. Consent rules may depend on a player's age, understanding, location and the purpose of collection.
